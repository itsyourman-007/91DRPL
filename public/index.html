Importance of Aerosol Management in Dental Clinics

Not Covid 19, Nor HMPV its tuberculosis that Indian Dentist needs to worry about.

The control of aerosols in dental clinics is paramount, particularly in the context of infectious disease transmission. Dental procedures often generate aerosols, which can harbor pathogens, presenting significant risks to both patients and dental professionals. The emergence of the COVID-19 pandemic underscored the importance of this issue, highlighting how easily viruses can be spread through respiratory droplets and aerosols during routine dental care. COVID-19, caused by the SARS-CoV-2 virus, demonstrated that dental settings could be hotspots for viral transmission, necessitating strict infection control protocols.

Additionally, tuberculosis (TB), a well-known airborne disease, poses ongoing challenges in dental environments. The Mycobacterium tuberculosis organism can survive in aerosolized droplets, presenting a risk of transmission during procedures, particularly in areas with higher prevalence. Ensuring proper aerosol management is critical to minimizing exposure.

To mitigate risks, dental clinics must adopt comprehensive infection control measures, including the use of high-efficiency particulate air (HEPA) filters, effective ventilation systems, and personal protective equipment (PPE) for staff and patients. Adhering to guidelines from public health organizations can further reduce aerosol generation and enhance safety. Ultimately, prioritizing aerosol control is essential for safeguarding health in dental clinics, fostering a safe environment for both practitioners and patients.

Dental Aerosol

What are Dental Aerosols?

Dental aerosols are the suspension of solid or liquid particles in the air generated by various dental procedures such as cleaning, polishing, drilling, and scaling. Dental procedures that involve high-speed equipment, such as handpieces, ultrasonic scalers, and air polishers, create a significant number of aerosols. Moreover, those created during restorative procedures, such as drilling, may contain bioaerosols comprising bacteria, viruses, and fungi, or patient-derived debris such as saliva, blood, and plaque.

How Dental Aerosols are harmful?

Dental aerosols became a significant concern during the COVID-19 pandemic peak due to the potential transmission of the virus via aerosols. When a patient has COVID-19, the virus can be transmitted through the aerosols during dental procedures, which can infect dental personnel. Besides COVID-19, dental aerosols can transmit other infectious agents such as tuberculosis, influenza, hepatitis, and more.

Contrary to what you may think, dental aerosols can remain airborne for hours, particularly if the room environment lacks proper ventilation. As a result, they can get inhaled by anyone present in the dental operatory, including the dental staff, caregivers, and even other patients. Moreover, when aerosols settle on surfaces, they can create a bioburden that may persist for some time and infect individuals who touch those surfaces.

Preventing Dental Aerosols

Dental professionals can take a variety of measures to reduce the spread of dental aerosols. Strategies to reduce aerosol production can consist of :

  1. Using high-volume evacuation systems (HVS), which are specifically designed to aspirate excessive aerosols and debris. They should be placed as close to the oral cavity as possible and switched on before initiating dental procedures. To make this HVS more effective, 91 Danta Research and Product Development Pvt Ltd, have innovated and made an attachment called DAB, (Dental Aerosol Blocker). DAB when attached to HVS, can be very effective in reducing the aerosol spread.
  2. Pre-procedural mouth rinses can reduce the number of viable microorganisms in aerosols.
  3. Use of protective barriers, such as a rubber dental dam. It can reduce aerosol production significantly by isolating the treated area.
  4. Proper ventilation and negative pressure rooms also help to minimize aerosol production in the dental operatory.
  5. Use of Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) such as N95 masks, face shields, goggles, and appropriate gowns can help protect healthcare workers from inhaling or coming into contact with infectious agents.

Conclusion

Even though, COVID 19 pandemic is not in the active phase and not many cases reported, dental aerosols are a significant concern and good habits in safe dental practices learnt in COVID can be continued. Dentists, hygienists, and other staff must be aware of their potential risks and take necessary measures to minimize them. The prudent implementation of protocols and guidelines can help minimize the risks associated with dental aerosols and ensure patient and clinician safety.
